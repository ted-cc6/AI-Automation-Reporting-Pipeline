Promoters most value the product's affordability and breadth: a low premium delivering a large, real benefit, whole-family coverage that other lenders do not offer, and a safety net that eases health emergencies. Detractors' main pain points cluster around product understanding, with many clients saying they were told they had insurance but never had the benefits or claim process explained, alongside coverage gaps on medicines and unresponsive service. The client protection picture is dominated by staff unresponsiveness and access barriers, and the remaining groups surfaced a claim reportedly rejected for a client and his daughter as well as a case where an account manager gave no follow-up on required medical studies. Looking forward, VisionFund should prioritize a structured, at-enrollment explanation of benefits and the claim process, paired with a reliable follow-up channel, to convert the large pool of confused-but-willing clients into confident users.
Top Findings
1. The single largest driver of dissatisfaction is a product understanding gap: clients repeatedly report being enrolled and charged without any explanation of the benefits or how to claim.
2. Promoters consistently praise the value equation, a small premium for large, whole-family coverage that they see as a genuine safety net unlike other insurers.
3. A recurring protection signal is unresponsive or absent staff follow-up when clients try to use the insurance, sometimes escalating to claims being told they are not valid.
Recommended Actions
4. Introduce a mandatory at-enrollment benefit and claims walkthrough with a take-home summary document, targeting the product understanding gap that dominates detractor and no-claim responses.
5. Establish and staff a reliable claims follow-up channel with response-time standards, since clients report calls going unanswered and claims stalling without follow-up.
6. Audit claim decisions for dependents and pre-existing conditions to ensure denials carry a documented valid reason, following the report of a client and his daughter being told their claim was not valid.

Part 1: Product Understanding & Awareness
Coverage Understanding
Coverage understanding across the LACRO portfolio was strong: 85.2% of the 1,721 surveyed clients understood what their policy covers. This result held almost identically across gender, with 85.2% of female clients and 85.4% of male clients demonstrating clear understanding. Among clients who experienced an insured event in the past twelve months, understanding was slightly higher for those who filed a claim, at 85.5%, than for those who experienced an event but did not file, at 81.2%. This suggests that navigating a claim reinforces clients' grasp of their coverage.
[VISUAL PENDING: part1_coverage_bar.png (Coverage Understanding by Segment)]
Claim Process Understanding
Understanding of the claims process was solid at 80.1% across the 1,721 surveyed clients, though somewhat lower than coverage understanding. Female clients reported 79.7% understanding and male clients 81.1%, a modest gap. The clearest divide appeared among clients who experienced an insured event in the past twelve months: 89.1% of those who filed a claim understood the process, compared with 63.8% of those who experienced an event but did not file. This gap indicates that direct experience with filing substantially strengthens process understanding, and points to an opportunity to reach clients before they need to claim.
[VISUAL PENDING: part1_claims_process_bar.png (Claims Process Understanding by Segment)]
Preferred Claims Channel
Asked which channel they would prefer for submitting a claim, clients across the portfolio favoured messaging: 42.4% (n=730) prefer WhatsApp or other messaging applications, and 31.3% (n=539) would like an MFI officer to collect their documents in person. A further 18.0% (n=310) prefer visiting a VisionFund office directly, while 3.1% (n=54) chose other channels and 2.6% (n=44) preferred email.
Product Value
Clients across the LACRO portfolio saw clear value in their coverage: 80.1% of the 1,721 surveyed clients agreed that their insurance was worth the premium they paid. This majority indicates that, despite gaps in how benefits and claims are explained at enrollment, most clients recognise the protection their policy provides as a worthwhile investment. Sustaining and building on this perceived value depends on strengthening the information clients receive when they first sign up.

Part 2: Claims Experience
Claims Funnel
Across the surveyed portfolio, 1,721 clients responded, and 124 (7.2%) reported experiencing an insured event in the past 12 months. Of those who experienced an event, 55 (44.4%) went on to file a claim, meaning roughly 55.6% of eligible clients did not file, a meaningful leakage from event to claim. Among the 55 filed claims, 49 (89.1%) were approved and paid. This funnel showed that once clients navigate to filing, the great majority reach a paid outcome; the sharpest fall-off occurs earlier, between experiencing an event and submitting a claim.

Reasons for Not Claiming
Among the 69 clients who experienced an insured event but did not file a claim, the leading reason was not knowing how to file (26, 37.7%), followed by "Other" (24, 34.8%) and the loss not being significant (12, 17.4%). Within the "Other" responses, nine clients forgot or were unaware of the coverage and five cited missing or incomplete documentation, pointing to awareness and process gaps rather than dissatisfaction.

Claim Challenges
Among the 11 clients reporting claim challenges, the most common was difficulty collecting documents from hospitals or authorities (6, 54.5%), followed by difficulty submitting documents (3, 27.3%) and long waits to receive the amount (1, 9.1%). Several protection flags warrant attention: a medium-severity mis-selling case where an expected benefit was not delivered, and four medium-severity staff-misconduct reports describing unanswered calls, no follow-up on required medical studies, and an unaddressed telephone-consultation request, each delaying a legitimate outcome.

Payout Outcomes

Part 3: Financial Inclusion
Reaching Clients with No Prior Insurance Access
For 77.2% of the 1,721 surveyed clients, VisionFund was their first insurer of any kind, extending financial protection to households that previously had no insurance at all. This reach was broadly balanced across gender, covering 76.3% of female clients and 79.3% of male clients. These figures show VisionFund bringing formal insurance protection to a large majority of households encountering it for the first time.
Financial Stress and Coping
Among the 124 clients who experienced an insured event in the last 12 months, whether or not they filed a claim, 6.5% reported resorting to at least one severe coping behavior. Separately, across all 1,721 surveyed clients, 1.7% reported high financial stress, an independent measure of the client base as a whole. Also across the full sample, 44.5% indicated that accessing alternative sources of financial support would be difficult, underscoring the value of reliable protection.

Confidence in Payout
Among all 1,721 surveyed clients, 84.7% expressed confidence that VisionFund will pay a valid claim. This high level of trust suggests that clients view the promise of protection as credible and dependable, an essential foundation for the value of insurance in previously underserved households.

Part 4: Client Voice
Net Promoter Score
The portfolio's Net Promoter Score reached 48.3 across 1,721 clients, with 60.7% promoters, 27.0% passives, and 12.3% detractors, revealing strong overall advocacy. Among clients who rated the premium as not worth it, the leading concern was that never having used the product left its value unproven, so they could not yet judge or trust it. The next most common theme involved unresponsive service and no follow-up, with clients who tried to use their insurance reporting little help or poor advisor attention when they needed it most.
[VISUAL PENDING: part4_nps.png (Net Promoter Score)]
Promoter and Detractor Themes
Promoters most often praised the product's value for money (264 mentions), followed by general satisfaction (256) and access and inclusion (184), showing that affordable, genuinely useful coverage anchors client advocacy. Detractors, by contrast, centred their comments on product understanding (83 mentions), general satisfaction (69), and improvement suggestions (49). The prominence of product understanding among detractors indicates that when clients struggle to grasp what their coverage does, dissatisfaction follows. This pattern suggests that clearer communication about benefits and use could convert uncertainty into confidence for a meaningful share of the portfolio.
[VISUAL PENDING: part4_themes.png (Top NPS Theme Drivers)]
Drivers of Satisfaction
Satisfaction, measured through NPS (48.3, n=1,721), was shaped most strongly by whether clients felt the premium was worth it (rho=-0.478, p<0.0001), where feeling the premium is worth it correlates with a higher NPS, a positive relationship. Confidence in receiving a payout followed closely (rho=-0.453, p<0.0001), with higher confidence aligning with higher NPS. Lower financial stress also tracked higher satisfaction (rho=-0.307, p<0.0001), as did better coverage understanding (rho=-0.244, p<0.0001) and clearer grasp of the claims process (rho=-0.213, p<0.0001). Every relationship was meaningful, pointing to perceived value and payout trust as central levers.

Gender analysis revealed largely comparable understanding of coverage between women and men (85.2% versus 85.4%, p=0.9089) and of the claim process (79.7% versus 81.1%, p=0.5067). Appendix: Methodology Notes
Spearman Rank Correlation (Parts 4 & 5 Drivers Tables)
ρ (rho) is the Spearman rank correlation coefficient, a value from -1 to +1 showing how strongly two things move together: values near +1 or -1 indicate a strong relationship, values near 0 indicate little to none. Spearman correlation is calculated on the rank order of responses rather than their raw values, which makes it well suited to Likert-scale survey questions like the ones in Parts 4 and 5. p (the p-value) shows how likely a correlation this strong could appear by chance alone if no real relationship existed; p<0.05 is the conventional threshold for statistical significance. N is the number of respondents included in each specific calculation, which can vary by driver because not every question was asked of every client.
Financial Stress, Coverage Understanding, Claim Process Understanding, Worth Premium, and Confidence in Payout are all scored so that a LOWER number is the more positive response (e.g. 1 = the most positive answer option for that question). A negative correlation with the outcome variable (client satisfaction, NPS, in Part 4; child wellbeing in Part 5) for any of these drivers therefore reflects a POSITIVE real-world relationship (e.g. a more positive response aligning with a better outcome), not a negative one. NPS itself runs the opposite way (0=worst, 10=best): when NPS appears as the outcome (Part 4), a negative correlation with it is what reflects the positive relationship described above; when NPS appears as a DRIVER of child wellbeing (Part 5 only), a positive correlation there likewise reflects a positive relationship.
